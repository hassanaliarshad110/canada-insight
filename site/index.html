Where amenities fall behind, under every assumption

Each score is a community's amenities within reach, per person who shares them, against Calgary's (1.00 = Calgary). It is worked out every reasonable way: both walking distances, all four ways of counting residents (the same as Calgary Data Desk), and, for the all-amenities score, two ways of weighting local amenities against district facilities. A consistent gap is below 0.80 in every one of them.

Which amenities are counted

By default, playgrounds and parks include the City's own and ones that are not the City's but open to everyone (mapped in OpenStreetMap: developer-held, school and community playgrounds and parks), with every one the City already lists taken out so nothing counts twice. This matches Calgary Data Desk. Use the filter on the community map to count the City's only, or to add private, residents-only ones (homeowners' association, condominium).

How this is measured

Every residential record on the city's property assessment roll is placed at the centre of its parcel. Purpose-built rental buildings, which the roll lists as one record, count as their number of units. For each kind of amenity, the straight-line distance from each home to the nearest one is measured, whichever community the amenity is in. Playgrounds and parks include ones open to everyone that the City doesn't list (from OpenStreetMap), unless you choose the City's only.

A community's figure is the share of its homes within the walking distance shown. Homes built since 2021 are measured separately, so you can see whether growth has been matched.

How to read a community

Measured gap
Most homes are farther than the walking distance from the nearest one in the inventory.
Check inventory
A measured gap where a quarter or more of homes are less than five years old. Developer-built parks and playgrounds may not be in the city's inventory yet; confirm on the ground before treating it as real.
Most homes within reach
Half or more of homes are within the walking distance.

Crowding

Nearness can't tell a playground shared by 80 children from one shared by 400. For playgrounds and parks, each one's children (or residents) within the walking distance are added up, its share is divided among them, and each home adds up the shares of everything within reach: playgrounds per 1,000 children within reach, and park hectares per 1,000 residents within reach. Children and residents come from Calgary Data Desk's estimates, spread evenly over each community's homes, under whichever of Calgary Data Desk's four ways of counting residents you choose (the same choice as the dashboard). These are estimates.

Scores

Each amenity's score is the community's crowding figure divided by Calgary's. Local amenities (playgrounds, parks, transit stops) and district facilities (community centres, recreation centres, libraries) are averaged separately, each amenity capped at 2.00 so a surplus can't hide a shortage. The all-amenities score weights everything equally, or local amenities at two thirds. Bands: under 0.50 well below Calgary, 0.50 to 0.80 below, 0.80 to 1.25 around, over 1.25 above.

No single figure is treated as the answer: the range across every way of working it out is shown, and only a gap that holds in every one is called a consistent gap. Straight-line distance is shorter than the walk itself, so these figures are the most generous reading.
