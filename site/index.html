Share of homes within walking distance, by community

How this is measured

Every residential record on the city's property assessment roll is placed at the centre of its parcel. Purpose-built rental buildings, which the roll lists as one record, count as their number of units. For each kind of amenity, the straight-line distance from each home to the nearest one in the city's inventory is measured, whichever community the amenity is in.

A community's figure is the share of its homes within the walking distance shown. Homes built since 2021 are measured separately, so you can see whether growth has been matched.

How to read a community

Measured gap
Most homes are farther than the walking distance from the nearest one in the inventory.
Check inventory
A measured gap where a quarter or more of homes are less than five years old. Developer-built parks and playgrounds may not be in the city's inventory yet; confirm on the ground before treating it as real.
Most homes within reach
Half or more of homes are within the walking distance.

Crowding

Nearness can't tell a playground shared by 80 children from one shared by 400. For playgrounds and parks, each one's children (or residents) within the walking distance are added up, its share is divided among them, and each home adds up the shares of everything within reach: playgrounds per 1,000 children within reach, and park hectares per 1,000 residents within reach. Children and residents come from Calgary Data Desk's estimates, spread evenly over each community's homes, under the growth model or the 2021 Census, whichever you choose. These are estimates.

There is no overall score: each amenity stands on its own. Straight-line distance is shorter than the walk itself, so these figures are the most generous reading.
